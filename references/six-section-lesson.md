# The Water Cycle

Audience: beginning learners, approximately ages 10-12.

## Section 1: The Water Cycle

Water moves continuously between Earth's surface and the atmosphere. Energy from the Sun and gravity help drive this movement. We call this ongoing movement the water cycle. There is no single starting point.

## Section 2: Evaporation

When liquid water gains enough energy, some water molecules escape into the air as water vapor. This is evaporation. Water vapor is an invisible gas; it should not be confused with the visible droplets in a cloud. Evaporation can happen below boiling temperature.

## Section 3: Condensation

When moist air cools enough, water vapor can condense into tiny liquid droplets. Many tiny droplets together can form a cloud. Cloud droplets are liquid water, not invisible water vapor.

## Section 4: Precipitation

Water droplets or ice particles in clouds can grow. When they become large enough to fall, water returns toward Earth's surface as precipitation. Rain is liquid precipitation; snow is made of ice crystals.

## Section 5: Runoff

Some precipitation flows over the land instead of soaking into it. Gravity moves this runoff downhill into streams and rivers. Rivers can carry water toward lakes and oceans.

## Section 6: Collection and Infiltration

Water collects in lakes and oceans. Some water also soaks into the ground, a process called infiltration, and can move through spaces in soil and rock. From these places, water can eventually return to the atmosphere, continuing the cycle.
